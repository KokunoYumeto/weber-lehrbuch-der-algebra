Then
\weq{8}{\begin{aligned}
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log f(\omega_1)\cdot f(\omega_2) &= -S_{00} + S_{10} + S_{01} - S_{11}, \\
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log f_1(\omega_1)\,f_1(\omega_2) &= -S_{00} + S_{10} - S_{01} + S_{11}, \\
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log f_2(\omega_1)\,f_2(\omega_2) &= -S_{00} - S_{10} + S_{01} + S_{11},
\end{aligned}}

from which by addition:
\[
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log 2 = -3\,S_{00} + S_{10} + S_{01} + S_{11},
\]
and if one eliminates $S_{00}$ by means of this:
\weq{9}{\begin{aligned}
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log f(\omega_1)\,f(\omega_2) &= \frac{2\pi}{3\,\beta}\log 2 + \tfrac{2}{3}S_{10} + \tfrac{2}{3}S_{01} - \tfrac{4}{3}S_{11}, \\
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log f_1(\omega_1)\,f_1(\omega_2) &= \frac{2\pi}{3\,\beta}\log 2 + \tfrac{2}{3}S_{10} - \tfrac{4}{3}S_{01} + \tfrac{2}{3}S_{11}, \\
\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\log f_2(\omega_1)\,f_2(\omega_2) &= \frac{2\pi}{3\,\beta}\log 2 - \tfrac{4}{3}S_{10} + \tfrac{2}{3}S_{01} + \tfrac{2}{3}S_{11}.
\end{aligned}}

These sums, however, are to be understood in such a way that in all of them at once
\[
(x-\alpha y)^2 + \beta^2 y^2 < n
\]
shall hold, and that $n$ then grows to infinity. Each individual sum $S$ thereby becomes
infinite, but their combinations, as they occur in these formulae, receive
finite limiting values.

We wish further to denote by $S'_{01}$, $S'_{10}$, $S'_{11}$ the sums which have the same
expression as $S_{01}$, $S_{10}$, $S_{11}$ according to (6), only with the difference \gesperrt{that $x$
and $y$ are to have no common divisor.}

If we set
\[
\begin{gathered}
S_{10} + S_{01} - 2\,S_{11} = T_n,\\
(x-\alpha y)^2 + \beta^2 y^2 < n,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Lim}}\limits_{n=\infty} T_n = T
\end{gathered}
\]
and denote by $T_n^{(p)}$ the sum which arises from $T_n$ when all terms
are removed in which $x$ and $y$ are both divisible by the odd prime $p$, then
there results:
\[
T_n^{(p)} = T_n - \frac{1}{p^2}\,T_{np^{-2}}
\]
and by passage to the limit $n=\infty$:
\[
T^{(p)} = T\left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right).
\]
