With regard to the formula Bd. I, § 145, (3), one may also give this relation the form:

\weq{24}{2^{\frac{n-1}{2}} \prod_{1,\,\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\nu} \vartheta_{00}\left(\frac{2\nu}{n}\right)\vartheta_{10}\left(\frac{2\nu}{n}\right)\vartheta_{01}\left(\frac{2\nu}{n}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{n^2-1}{8}} \vartheta_{00}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\vartheta_{10}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\vartheta_{01}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.}

\begin{center}
\textbf{\S\,33. \gesperrt{The Principal Transformations of Odd Order.}}
\end{center}

The formula last proved is of use to us in carrying out the transformation of odd order $n$. We consider first the \gesperrt{first principal transformation}. By § 27, (12),

\weq{1}{\vartheta_{11}(nu,\,n\omega)}

is a $\Theta_{11}$-function of order $n$ in $u$ and $\omega$, and this can easily be formed from its zero points, of which there are $n$ in the period parallelogram. The zero points of (1) are the values
\[
u = \frac{\nu + \mu n\omega}{n} = \frac{\nu}{n} + \mu\omega,
\]
when $\nu$ and $\mu$ are integers, and one obtains all the incongruent ones among these values by holding $\mu$ fixed and letting $\nu$ run through a complete system of residues with respect to the modulus $n$. We choose the system of residues
\[
0,\ \pm 1,\ \pm 2,\ \ldots,\ \pm\frac{(n-1)}{2},
\]
and accordingly obtain, if $C$ denotes a factor independent of $u$,

\weq{2}{C\vartheta_{11}(nu,\,n\omega) = \vartheta_{11}(u) \prod_{1,\,\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\nu} \vartheta_{11}\left(\frac{\nu}{n} + u\right)\vartheta_{11}\left(\frac{\nu}{n} - u\right),}

a formula which, by § 22, (4), can also be expressed in the following way through the functions $\vartheta_{11}(u)$, $\vartheta_{01}(u)$:
\[
C\vartheta_{01}^{n-1}\vartheta_{11}(nu,\,n\omega) = \vartheta_{11}(u) \prod_{1,\,\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\nu} \left[\vartheta_{11}^2\left(\frac{\nu}{n}\right)\vartheta_{01}^2(u) - \vartheta_{01}^2\left(\frac{\nu}{n}\right)\vartheta_{11}^2(u)\right].
\]
We replace $u$ by
\[
u + \frac{1}{2}, \quad u + \frac{\omega}{2}, \quad u + \frac{1+\omega}{2}
\]
