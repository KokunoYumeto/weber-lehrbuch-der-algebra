Let $\alpha_0, \beta_0$ be the finite values of the functions $\alpha, \beta$ at a point $\mathfrak{P}$. Then $F(\alpha_0, \beta_0) = 0$, and we can represent equation (1) in the following way:
\weq{3}{
\begin{aligned}
0 = {} & (\alpha - \alpha_0)F'(\alpha_0) + (\beta - \beta_0)F'(\beta_0) \\
& + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[(\alpha-\alpha_0)^2 F''(\alpha_0,\alpha_0) + 2(\alpha-\alpha_0)(\beta-\beta_0)F''(\alpha_0,\beta_0) \right. \\
& \left. \quad + (\beta-\beta_0)^2 F''(\beta_0,\beta_0)\right] + \cdots
\end{aligned}
}
The two mutually reciprocal functions
\[
\frac{\alpha - \alpha_0}{\beta - \beta_0}, \quad \frac{\beta - \beta_0}{\alpha - \alpha_0}
\]
cannot both be infinite at the point $\mathfrak{P}$. Let therefore:
\[
\left(\frac{\alpha - \alpha_0}{\beta - \beta_0}\right)_0
\]
be the finite value of the first of these quotients. Then it follows from (3), if $F'(\alpha_0)$ is not zero:
\weq{4}{\left(\frac{\alpha - \alpha_0}{\beta - \beta_0}\right)_0 = -\frac{F'(\beta_0)}{F'(\alpha_0)} = -\left(\frac{F'(\beta)}{F'(\alpha)}\right)_0 = \left(\frac{d\alpha}{d\beta}\right)_0.}
This equation holds for all points $\mathfrak{P}$, with the possible exception of a finite number\footnote{Among the points to be excepted are in any case those in which $\alpha_0$ or $\beta_0$ becomes infinite. For at these points the functions $\alpha - \alpha_0$, $\beta - \beta_0$ are not defined at all.}.

1. \gesperrt{A function $\eta$ in $\Omega$ which at all points, with the exception of a finite number, satisfies the condition:}
\weq{5}{\eta_0 = \left(\frac{\alpha-\alpha_0}{\beta-\beta_0}\right)_0}
\gesperrt{is identical with $\left(\dfrac{d\alpha}{d\beta}\right)$.}

For from (4) and (5) it follows that the function
\[
\left(\frac{d\alpha}{d\beta}\right) - \eta
\]
has infinitely many zero points and must therefore vanish identically.

Formula (5) can accordingly likewise serve as the definition of the differential quotient.
