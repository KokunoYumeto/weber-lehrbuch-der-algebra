\begin{center}
\gesperrt{Twenty-seventh Chapter.}

\textbf{Algebraic and Abelian Differentials.}

\rule{0.3\linewidth}{0.4pt}

\textbf{\S\,197. \gesperrt{Differentials in $\Omega$.}}
\end{center}

If $z$ and $z_1$ are two variables in $\Omega$ of the orders $n$, $n_1$ with the lower vertices $\mathfrak{U}$, $\mathfrak{U}_1$, with the branching vertices: $\mathfrak{Z}$, $\mathfrak{Z}_1$, and the branching numbers $w$, $w_1$, then by \S\ 195, (12)
\[
\dfrac{dz}{dz_1} = \dfrac{\mathfrak{Z}\mathfrak{U}_1^2}{\mathfrak{Z}_1\mathfrak{U}^2}.
\]

If one sets
\weq{1}{\omega = \dfrac{\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{U}^2}{\mathfrak{B}\mathfrak{Z}}, \quad \omega_1 = \dfrac{\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{U}_1^2}{\mathfrak{B}\mathfrak{Z}_1},}
then it follows from this that
\weq{2}{\omega\,dz = \omega_1\,dz_1.}

If $\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{U}^2$ is equivalent to $\mathfrak{B}\mathfrak{Z}$, then $\omega$ is a function in $\Omega$, and $\omega_1$ is likewise a function in $\Omega$. If $a$, $b$ are the orders of $\mathfrak{A}$ and $\mathfrak{B}$, then [\S\ 196, (2)]
\weq{3}{\begin{aligned}
b+w &= a+2n,\\
a &= b+2p-2,
\end{aligned}}
where $p$ is the genus of the field $\Omega$.

We now set, in a new symbolic notation,
\weq{4}{dz = \dfrac{\mathfrak{Z}}{\mathfrak{U}^2},}
\weq{5}{\omega\,dz = dJ = \dfrac{\mathfrak{A}}{\mathfrak{B}},}
and we call these expressions the \gesperrt{differentials} belonging to the field $\Omega$.

In the numerator and denominator $\mathfrak{A}$, $\mathfrak{B}$ of a differential, common factors may be added or omitted. If $\mathfrak{A}$ and $\mathfrak{B}$ have no common divisor, then $\mathfrak{A}$ is called the \gesperrt{upper vertex}, $\mathfrak{B}$ the \gesperrt{lower vertex} of the differential $dJ$.
