\begin{center}
\textbf{§ 57. \gesperrt{Multiplication of the Elliptic Functions.}}
\end{center}

By the multiplication of the elliptic functions one understands the
representation of the functions $\mathrm{sn}\,nv$, $\mathrm{cn}\,nv$,
$\mathrm{dn}\,nv$ for an integer $n$ as rational functions of
$\mathrm{sn}\,v$, $\mathrm{cn}\,v$, $\mathrm{dn}\,v$ --- a problem
which, as is evident from the addition theorem, can always be solved.

The form of the solution follows easily from the consideration of the
$\vartheta$-functions.

As is to be seen immediately from § 21, the functions
$\vartheta_{g_1,g_2}(nu)$ are $\Theta$-functions of order $n^2$, whose
characteristic is equal to $(0,0)$ for even $n$ and equal to
$(g_1,g_2)$ for odd $n$. Moreover $\vartheta_{11}(nu)$ is an odd
function, while $\vartheta_{00}(nu)$, $\vartheta_{10}(nu)$,
$\vartheta_{01}(nu)$ are even functions of $u$. These functions can
therefore be represented rationally by the $\vartheta$-functions, and
the theorems of § 21 yield the form of these expressions. If we again
denote by $F^{(\nu)}(x,y)$ an integral, rational, homogeneous function
of order $\nu$, we obtain:

\gesperrt{for even $n$:}

\weq{1}{\begin{aligned}
\vartheta_{11}(nu) &= \vartheta_{00}(u)\vartheta_{01}(u)\vartheta_{10}(u)\vartheta_{11}(u)\,F^{\frac{n^2-4}{2}}\bigl[\vartheta_{11}^2(u),\vartheta_{01}^2(u)\bigr],\\
\vartheta_{g_1,g_2}(nu) &= F^{\frac{n^2}{2}}\bigl[\vartheta_{11}^2(u),\vartheta_{01}^2(u)\bigr], \quad (g_1,g_2)=(10),(01),(00),
\end{aligned}}

\gesperrt{for odd $n$:}

\weq{2}{\vartheta_{g_1,g_2}(nu) = \vartheta_{g_1,g_2}(u)\,F^{\frac{n^2-1}{2}}\bigl[\vartheta_{11}^2(u),\vartheta_{01}^2(u)\bigr].}
