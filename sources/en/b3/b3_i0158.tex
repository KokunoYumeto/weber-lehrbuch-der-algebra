If \(\omega\) is the modulus of the \(\vartheta\)-functions, then, as follows by
(1) from the vanishing of the four \(\vartheta\)-functions, the following values
belong together:
\weq{11}{\begin{aligned}
u &= \tfrac{1}{2}\omega, & \xi : \eta &= \xi_1 : \eta_1,\\
u &= 0, & \xi : \eta &= \xi_2 : \eta_2,\\
u &= \tfrac{1}{2}, & \xi : \eta &= \xi_3 : \eta_3,\\
u &= \frac{1+\omega}{2}, & \xi : \eta &= \xi_4 : \eta_4.
\end{aligned}}

\begin{center}
\textbf{\S\,42. \gesperrt{Jacobi's elliptic functions.}}
\end{center}

Since the variables \(\xi\), \(\eta\) can be replaced by two new variables by
means of a linear substitution in which four coefficients are at our
disposal, one can assign arbitrary values to four of the quantities \(\xi_i\),
\(\eta_i\), or to three of their ratios, without impairing the
generality.

We shall set
\weq{1}{\xi_1 = 0,\qquad \eta_2 = 0,\qquad \xi_3 = \eta_3,}
and we further introduce \(\varkappa^2\), \(\varkappa'^2\), \(\zeta\) by the equations:
\weq{2}{\xi_4 = \varkappa^2\eta_4,\qquad \varkappa'^2 = 1 - \varkappa^2,\qquad \frac{\eta}{\xi} = \zeta.}
