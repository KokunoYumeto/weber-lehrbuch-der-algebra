\begin{center}
\textbf{\S\,41. \gesperrt{Connection of the \(\vartheta\)-functions with the elliptic\\
integrals.}}
\end{center}

According to \S\,21 there exist between the squares of the four
\(\vartheta\)-functions two linear equations independent of one another, and
one can therefore express two of these squares by the other two, or indeed
all four by two independent variables \(\xi\), \(\eta\). In doing the latter,
we denote by \(\xi_1\), \(\eta_1\); \(\xi_2\), \(\eta_2\); \(\xi_3\), \(\eta_3\); \(\xi_4\), \(\eta_4\)
constants, and, connecting with the mode of notation of Bd.\,I, \S\,67, we
set:
\weq{1}{\begin{aligned}
\vartheta^2_{01}(u) &= \xi\eta_1 - \eta\xi_1 = (\xi\eta_1),\\
\vartheta^2_{11}(u) &= \xi\eta_2 - \eta\xi_2 = (\xi\eta_2),\\
\vartheta^2_{10}(u) &= \xi\eta_3 - \eta\xi_3 = (\xi\eta_3),\\
\vartheta^2_{00}(u) &= \xi\eta_4 - \eta\xi_4 = (\xi\eta_4).
\end{aligned}}
Between the constants \(\xi_i\), \(\eta_i\) and the values \(\vartheta_{01}\), \(\vartheta_{10}\),
\(\vartheta_{00}\) there exist four relations, which can be derived from the
equations (13) of \S\,21. These equations we can write, following the
notation (1), in the form:
\weq{2}{\begin{aligned}
(\xi\eta_3)\vartheta^2_{01} &= (\xi\eta_1)\vartheta^2_{10} - (\xi\eta_2)\vartheta^2_{00},\\
(\xi\eta_4)\vartheta^2_{01} &= (\xi\eta_1)\vartheta^2_{00} - (\xi\eta_2)\vartheta^2_{10}.
\end{aligned}}
By setting in (2) \(\xi\), \(\eta = \xi_1\), \(\eta_1\)
and \(= \xi_2\), \(\eta_2\), one can give these relations the form:
\weq{3}{\begin{aligned}
(\xi_1\eta_3)\vartheta^2_{01} &= (\xi_2\eta_1)\vartheta^2_{00},\\
(\xi_2\eta_3)\vartheta^2_{01} &= (\xi_2\eta_1)\vartheta^2_{10},\\
(\xi_1\eta_4)\vartheta^2_{01} &= (\xi_2\eta_1)\vartheta^2_{10},\\
(\xi_2\eta_4)\vartheta^2_{01} &= (\xi_2\eta_1)\vartheta^2_{00},
\end{aligned}}
to which one can further add, by setting in (2) \(\xi\), \(\eta = \xi_4\), \(\eta_4\)
and using (3) and \S\,21, (14):
\weq{4}{(\xi_4\eta_3) = (\xi_2\eta_1).}
