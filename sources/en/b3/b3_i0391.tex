If we denote by $A$, $B$, $C$ the classes in $O$ to which $\lambda$, $\mu$, $\nu$ belong, and likewise denote the classes of the quadratic forms $U$, $V$, $T$, then $C$ is also said to be \gesperrt{compounded} from $A$ and $B$, and one sets symbolically
\weq{8}{C = AB.}
\gesperrt{This composition of the classes} is uniquely determined once any three representatives $U$, $V$, $T$ of them are given, and it is therefore enough if we choose $U$, $V$ in their classes in any suitable way.

In every ideal class there are ideals that are relatively prime to any given ideal. We therefore first take $\mathfrak{a}$ in $A$ primary, but otherwise arbitrary, then $\mathfrak{b}$ likewise primary and relatively prime, not only to $\mathfrak{a}$, but to $a_1$. Then $a_1$ and $a_2$ are relatively prime (for the greatest common divisor $d$ of $a_1$ and $a_2$ would be relatively prime to $\mathfrak{b}$, and consequently $a_2 : d$ would be divisible by $\mathfrak{b}$). But from this it follows that $d=1$, because $a_2$ was to be the least natural number divisible by $\mathfrak{b}$.

Once $a_1$, $a_2$ have been determined in this way, one can determine $b$ in accordance with the two congruences
\[
\begin{aligned}
b &\equiv b_1 \pmod{2 a_1}, \\
&\equiv b_2 \pmod{2 a_2}
\end{aligned}
\]
and then
\[
b^2 - D = 4\,a_1 a_2 c
\]
is divisible by $4 a_1 a_2$. We therefore set
\weq{9}{
\begin{aligned}
U &= (a_1,\ b,\ a_2 c), \\
V &= (a_2,\ b,\ a_1 c).
\end{aligned}
}
Now $a_1 a_2$ is the least rational integer divisible by $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}$, and $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}$ is the greatest common divisor of
\[
a_1 a_2, \quad \frac{b-\sqrt D}{2};
\]
consequently
\weq{10}{T = (a_1 a_2,\ b,\ c)}
is the form compounded from $U$ and $V$, and the linear forms $\lambda$, $\mu$, $\nu$ corresponding to the quadratic forms $T$, $U$, $V$ are:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= a_1 a_2\,t_1 + \frac{b-\sqrt D}{2}\,t_2, \\
\mu &= a_1\,u_1 + \frac{b-\sqrt D}{2}\,u_2, \\
\nu &= a_2\,v_1 + \frac{b-\sqrt D}{2}\,v_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
