One can therefore replace every fractional function of $\theta$ by an integral function of $\theta$, and so obtains the theorem:

1. \gesperrt{Every function $\zeta$ of the field $\Omega$ can be put in one single way into the form:}
\weq{6}{\zeta = x_0 + x_1\theta + x_2\theta^2 + \cdots + x_{n-1}\theta^{n-1},}
\gesperrt{whose coefficients $x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{n-1}$ are rational functions of $z$.}

That this is possible at all is inferred from (4) and (5); that it is possible in only one way follows from the irreducibility of $f(\theta)$; for if one had two different representations of one and the same quantity $\zeta$ in the form (6), their difference would yield an equation for $\theta$ of a degree lower than $n$.

If one chooses $n$ functions of the field $\Omega$ arbitrarily:
\weq{7}{
\begin{aligned}
\eta_1 &= x_0^{(1)} + x_1^{(1)}\theta + \cdots + x_{n-1}^{(1)}\theta^{n-1}, \\
\eta_2 &= x_0^{(2)} + x_2^{(2)}\theta + \cdots + x_{n-1}^{(2)}\theta^{n-1}, \\% [sic] printed x_2^{(2)}, not x_1^{(2)}
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots \\
\eta_n &= x_0^{(n)} + x_1^{(n)}\theta + \cdots + x_{n-1}^{(n)}\theta^{n-1},
\end{aligned}
}
with the single restriction that the determinant
\[
\Sigma \pm x_0^{(1)} x_1^{(2)} \cdots x_{n-1}^{(n)}
\]
is not identically zero, then by elimination of the powers of $\theta$ from (6) and (7) every function $\zeta$ in $\Omega$ can be put into the form:
\weq{8}{\zeta = y_1\eta_1 + y_2\eta_2 + \cdots + y_n\eta_n,}
whose coefficients $y_i$ are rational functions of $z$.

Such a system of functions $\eta_1, \eta_2, \ldots, \eta_n$ is called a \gesperrt{basis of the field} $\Omega$.

In order that a system of functions $\eta_1, \eta_2, \ldots, \eta_n$ form a basis of $\Omega$, it is necessary and sufficient that no equation of the form
\[
y_1\eta_1 + y_2\eta_2 + \cdots + y_n\eta_n = 0
\]
subsist in which the coefficients $y_1, y_2, \ldots, y_n$ do not all vanish identically.

Thus the functions
\[
1, \theta, \theta^2, \ldots, \theta^{n-1}
\]
form a basis of $\Omega$.
