every substitution $L$ of the group $\mathfrak{L}$ can be composed. First of
all we have
\weq{3}{A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}1, & 0\\-1, & 1\end{pmatrix}, \quad A^\lambda = \begin{pmatrix}1, & 0\\\lambda, & 1\end{pmatrix}}
(for every positive or negative integer $\lambda$)
\weq{4}{B^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}0, & -1\\1, & 0\end{pmatrix}, \quad B^2 = \begin{pmatrix}-1, & 0\\0, & -1\end{pmatrix}, \quad B^3 = B^{-1}.}

We further put
\weq{5}{C = \begin{pmatrix}1, & -1\\0, & 1\end{pmatrix} = B^{-1}AB, \quad C^\lambda = \begin{pmatrix}1, & -\lambda\\0, & 1\end{pmatrix},}
$C$ is therefore derivable from $A$ and $B$.

Now let $L = \begin{pmatrix}\alpha, & \beta\\\gamma, & \delta\end{pmatrix}$ be an arbitrary linear transformation.
From it we derive the sequence:
\[
L' = LA^\lambda, \quad L'' = L'C^{\lambda'}, \quad L''' = L''A^{\lambda''}, \quad L'''' = L'''C^{\lambda'''},
\]
whose first and second elements are formed thus:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\alpha' = \alpha + \lambda\beta, \quad \beta'' = \beta' - \lambda'\alpha', \quad \alpha''' = \alpha'' + \lambda''\beta'',\\
\beta'''' = \beta''' - \lambda'''\alpha''', \dots,
\end{gathered}
\]
and one may dispose of $\lambda, \lambda', \lambda'', \lambda''', \dots$ in such a way that, in
absolute value,
\[
\alpha' \leqq \tfrac{1}{2}\beta, \quad \beta'' \leqq \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha', \quad \alpha''' \leqq \tfrac{1}{2}\beta'', \quad \beta'''' \geqq \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha''' \dots, % [sic] Printed \geqq; the stated decreasing sequence requires \leqq.
\]
as long as none of these numbers vanishes. The numbers
\[
\beta, \alpha', \beta'', \alpha''', \beta'''', \dots
\]
therefore form a sequence of numbers decreasing in absolute value, and after a finite number of compositions
of this kind a number of this sequence must vanish.

If $\beta^{(\nu)} = 0$, then
\[
L^{(\nu)} = \begin{pmatrix}\pm 1, & 0\\\gamma^{(\nu)}, & \pm 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\pm 1, & 0\\0, & \pm 1\end{pmatrix} A^{\pm\gamma^{(\nu)}}
\]
and if $\alpha^{(\nu)} = 0$, then
\[
L^{(\nu)} = \begin{pmatrix}0, & \pm 1\\\mp 1, & \delta^{(\nu)}\end{pmatrix} = A^{\pm\delta^\nu} B^{\pm 1},
% [sic] German source prints \delta^\nu here; the parallel matrix entry has \delta^{(\nu)}.
\]
and since
\[
\begin{gathered}
L = L'A^{-\lambda} = L''C^{-\lambda'}A^{-\lambda}\\
= L'''A^{-\lambda''}C^{-\lambda'}A^{-\lambda} = L''''C^{-\lambda'''}A^{-\lambda''}C^{-\lambda'}A^{-\lambda}, \dots
\end{gathered}
\]
holds, the theorem is proved.
