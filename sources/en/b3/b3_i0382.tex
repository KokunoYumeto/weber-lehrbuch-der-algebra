\begin{center}
\S\,99. \gesperrt{Equivalence in the Orders.}
\end{center}

10. \gesperrt{Definition. We will now call two ideals $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{a}'$ equivalent with respect to the order $[Q]$ if}
\weq{1}{\mathfrak{a}' = \eta' \mathfrak{a}}
\gesperrt{holds, and $\eta'$ denotes a number in $O'$. $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{a}'$ are here always assumed to be coprime to $Q$.}

If one takes a number $\mu = \mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{m}$ in $[Q]$ divisible by $\mathfrak{a}$ in such a way that $\mathfrak{m}$ becomes a primary ideal, and sets $\mu' = \eta'\mu$, then we have:
\weq{2}{\begin{aligned}\mu&=\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a},\\ \mu'&=\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}',\end{aligned}}
\gesperrt{and we can also explain the equivalence of $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{a}'$ with respect to $[Q]$ by the fact that $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{a}'$ are transformed into numbers of the order $[Q]$ by multiplication with one and the same ideal $\mathfrak{m}$.}

By the ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ there is determined, according to \S\,97, a family of parallel quadratic forms $(a,b,c)$\footnote{By a family of parallel forms one understands the system \[ (a,\ b+2la,\ c+lb+l^2a), \] when $l$ runs through all rational integers.}, in which $a$ and $b$ are determined from % [sic] German source prints singular wird with plural a und b.

\weq{3}{a = N(\mathfrak{m}),\quad \frac{b+\sqrt{D}}{2} \equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{m})}
And so this family of forms does not change, by (2), when $a$ is replaced by an equivalent ideal $a'$.

But if in (2) one takes another ideal $\mathfrak{m}_1$ in place of the ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ and sets
\weq{4}{\mu_1 = \mathfrak{m}_1\mathfrak{a},}
then
\[
\frac{\mu}{\mu_1} = \frac{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{m}_1} = \eta
\]
is a number in $[O']$, and hence $\mathfrak{m}_1$ is equivalent to $\mathfrak{m}$.
