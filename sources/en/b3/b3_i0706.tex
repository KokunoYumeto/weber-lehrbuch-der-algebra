In a family of dimension $s > 1$

\weq{12}{S = (\mathfrak{A}_1, \mathfrak{A}_2, \ldots, \mathfrak{A}_s)}

one can find a polygon that contains an arbitrary point $\mathfrak{P}$ at least
once more often than the divisor $\mathfrak{M}$ of this family does.

This purpose is attained by setting up the development (2) for this
point and then determining one of the constants $c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_s$
from the equation

\weq{13}{c_1e_1 + c_2e_2 + \cdots + c_se_s = 0}

These polygons then form a family of the $(s-1)$th
dimension, whose divisor is divisible by $\mathfrak{M}\mathfrak{P}$.

This theorem can be extended as follows:

5. \gesperrt{The polygons of an $s$-fold family that are divisible
by an $r$-gon $\mathfrak{R}$ form an at least
$(s-r)$-fold family.}

If this theorem has already been proved for an $r$-gon, it follows
for an $(r+1)$-gon $\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{P}$ from the preceding one.
For by the
addition of the point $\mathfrak{P}$ the dimension is either not
changed, if $\mathfrak{P}$ divides the divisor of the family reduced by $\mathfrak{R}$,
or it is diminished by 1.

The polygon $\mathfrak{R}$ can be so chosen that the dimension of
$S$ is reduced exactly to $(s-r)$. For this purpose the
points of $\mathfrak{R}$ have to be chosen successively so that each following one
is not contained in the divisor of the family reduced by the
preceding ones. From this it follows:
