If the four transformation numbers have a common factor, then
this factor can be separated off by means of the formula
\[
\begin{pmatrix}m, & 0\\0, & m\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a, & b\\c, & \partial\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}ma, & mb\\mc, & m\partial\end{pmatrix}
\]
through composition with the multiplication, and we accordingly
now assume that $a, b, c, \partial$ \gesperrt{have no common
divisor}. One can then always determine the two
integers $\xi, \eta$ in such a way that
\weq{3}{\begin{gathered} a\eta - c\xi = \alpha\\ b\eta - \partial\xi = \beta \end{gathered}}
are without a common divisor.

To see this, we first assume $\xi, \eta$ to be relatively prime.
Then every common divisor of $\alpha, \beta$ is necessarily a
divisor of $n$, as the solutions of (3)
\weq{4}{\begin{gathered} n\xi = b\alpha - a\beta\\ n\eta = \partial\alpha - c\beta \end{gathered}}
show. If one therefore takes $\xi$ \gesperrt{not divisible} by all the primes
that divide $a$ and $b$ at once, but $\xi$ \gesperrt{divisible} and
$\eta$ \gesperrt{indivisible} by all the other primes that divide $n$,
and moreover $\xi, \eta$ relatively prime, which is always possible, then
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ have no common divisor. Thereupon one determines $\gamma, \delta$
so that
\[
\alpha\delta - \beta\gamma = 1.
\]

By (3) it is then also the case that
\[
(a\delta - b\gamma)\eta - (c\delta - \partial\gamma)\xi = 1,
\]
and the following composition results, as is easily recognised by
use of (4):
\weq{5}{\begin{pmatrix}a, & b\\c, & \partial\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}a\delta - b\gamma, & \xi\\c\delta - \partial\gamma, & \eta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1, & 0\\0, & n\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha, & \beta\\\gamma, & \delta\end{pmatrix}.}

If we therefore call
\weq{6}{\begin{pmatrix}1, & 0\\0, & n\end{pmatrix}}
the \gesperrt{principal transformation} of degree $n$, then it is thereby proved
\gesperrt{that all transformations of degree $n$ can be composed of a
multiplication, a principal transformation and linear
transformations.}
