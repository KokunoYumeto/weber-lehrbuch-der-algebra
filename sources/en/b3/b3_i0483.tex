and of the quantities (11) just as many drop out, as not being solutions of (3), as the congruence (13) has solutions. If for the moment we denote the number of these solutions by $t$, then the number of the values of $v$ that belong to the same value of $u$ is $(p + 1 - t)$.

If we first take $p = 2$, then, since $A$ is odd, we have the following cases:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B &\equiv 0, & & & c &\equiv C \pmod{2}, & t &= 1, \\
B &\equiv 1, & C &\equiv 0, & c &\equiv 0, 1 \pmod{2}, & t &= 2, \\
B &\equiv 1, & C &\equiv 1 & & \pmod{2}, & t &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\]
(because $c^2 - c$ is always even). This may be summarized as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
D &\equiv 0 \pmod{4}, & t &= 1, \\
D &\equiv 1 \pmod{8}, & t &= 2, \\
D &\equiv 5 \pmod{8}, & t &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using the symbol $(D, p)$ (\S\,85) we may therefore put:
\weq{14}{t = (D, p) + 1.}
If $p$ is odd, the congruence (13) is equivalent to
\[
(2Ac - B)^2 \equiv D \pmod{p},
\]
and this has one solution ($2Ac - B \equiv 0$) when $p$ divides $D$, that is, when $(D, p) = 0$; two solutions when $D$ is a quadratic residue of $p$, that is, when $(D, p) = +1$; and no solution when $D$ is a quadratic non-residue, that is, when $(D, p) = -1$. So formula (14) holds here also.
