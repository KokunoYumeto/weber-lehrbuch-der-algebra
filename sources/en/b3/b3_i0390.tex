If we therefore set
\[
\lambda_1 = a_1 t_1 + \frac{b+\sqrt D}{2}\,t_2,
\]
then
\[
N(\lambda_1) = a_1 (a_1 t_1^2 + b\,t_1 t_2 + a_2 c\,t_2^2),
\]
and $(a_1,\ b,\ a_2 c)$ is likewise primitive. Since $\lambda_1$ is now divisible by $\mathfrak{a}$, $a_1$ must be divisible by $N(\mathfrak{a})$, i.\,e. by $a$, and must therefore be $=a$. Hence $\mathfrak{a}$ is primary.

If in (1) we fix the $\sqrt D$ once and for all, e.\,g. as positively real or positively imaginary, then the linear form $\lambda$ is \gesperrt{uniquely determined} by the quadratic form $T$. The form of the conjugate ideal would be obtained not by changing the sign of $\sqrt D$, but that of $b$.

Let $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ be any two ideals coprime to the conductor $Q$ of the order $[Q]$, which we shall now denote by $O$, and let
\weq{5}{
\begin{aligned}
\mu &= \alpha_1 u_1 + \alpha_2 u_2,\\
\nu &= \beta_1 v_1 + \beta_2 v_2
\end{aligned}
}
be basis forms of $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ in $O$. From $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ we form the product
\[
\mathfrak{a}\,\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{c}
\]
with the basis form in $O$:
\weq{6}{\lambda = \gamma_1 t_1 + \gamma_2 t_2.}
The quadratic forms belonging to $\mu$, $\nu$, $\lambda$ shall be denoted by $U$, $V$, $T$.

1. \gesperrt{We call $\lambda$ composed or compounded out of $\mu$ and $\nu$, and $T$ out of $U$ and $V$.}

We set:
\weq{7}{
\begin{aligned}
T &= (a,\ b,\ c),\\
U &= (a_1,\ b_1,\ c_1),\\
V &= (a_2,\ b_2,\ c_2),
\end{aligned}
}
and our problem is solved if $T$ can be derived from $U$ and $V$.

If
\[
\begin{array}{lcl}
\mathfrak{a} & \text{equivalent to} & \mathfrak{a}', \\
\mathfrak{b} & \text{\textquotedbl}\quad\text{\textquotedbl} & \mathfrak{b}',
\end{array}
\]
then also
\[
\mathfrak{a}\,\mathfrak{b} \quad \text{\textquotedbl}\quad\text{\textquotedbl} \quad \mathfrak{a}'\mathfrak{b}',
\]
hence
\[
\mathfrak{c} \quad \text{\textquotedbl}\quad\text{\textquotedbl} \quad \mathfrak{c}',
\]
and if therefore $U$, $V$ are replaced by equivalent forms $U'$, $V'$, then an equivalent form $T'$ also takes the place of $T$.
