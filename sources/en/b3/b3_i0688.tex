6. \gesperrt{If $e_1 = 1$, then $F'(\tau)$ is not divisible by
$\pi_1$. Otherwise $F'(\tau)$ is divisible by $\pi_1^{e_1-1}$, but not
by $\pi_1^{e_1}$. The same holds of the remaining prime factors of
$(z-c)$, and consequently $F'(\tau)$ is coprime to all prime factors
$z-c$ in $Z$ which are not divisible by the square of a prime factor
in $\Omega$.}

There is therefore only a finite number of prime factors $(z-c)$ that
are divisible by a higher than the first power of a prime factor in
$\Omega$, and $F'(\tau)$, decomposed into prime factors, is the
product of all the factors $\pi^{e-1}$, when $\pi^e$ is the highest
power of $\pi$ dividing $N(\pi) = z - c$.

The functional $F'(\tau)$ is called the \gesperrt{ramification functional} of the
field $\Omega$.

For the absolute norm of the ramification functional we obtain:

\weq{24}{N_a F'(\tau) = \Pi (z-c)^{e_1-1+e_2-1+\cdots},}

in which the product $\Pi$ extends over all linear factors $(z-c)$
that are divisible by a higher than the first power of a prime
factor.

We can show further that this absolute norm is nothing other than the
\gesperrt{field discriminant} $D$.

In order to prove this, we set:

\weq{25}{\tau^k = u_{k,1}\omega_1 + u_{k,2}\omega_2 + \cdots + u_{k,n}\omega_n,}
% [sic] printed u_{k,1}\omega_1 + ... + u_{k,n}\omega_n (and u_{k,1}, ..., u_{k,n} in the next sentence) with the power index k first, inconsistent with (26) and the congruence below, which put the power index second (u_{1,0}, ..., u_{n,n-1}; u_{r,0}y_0 + ...)

in which the $u_{k,1}, u_{k,2}, \ldots, u_{k,n}$ are homogeneous
functions of degree $k$ in $t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_n$, and the
determinant

\weq{26}{U =
\begin{vmatrix}
u_{1,0}, & u_{2,0}, & \ldots, & u_{n,0} \\
u_{1,1}, & u_{2,1}, & \ldots, & u_{n,1} \\
\cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\
u_{1,n-1}, & u_{2,n-1}, & \ldots, & u_{n,n-1}
\end{vmatrix}}

is a unit.

For if $U$ were not a unit, it would have at least one linear factor
$z-c$, and one could determine the integral rational holomorphic
functionals $y_0, y_1, \ldots, y_{n-1}$ in such a way that

\[ u_{r,0}y_0 + u_{r,1}y_1 + \cdots + u_{r,n-1}y_{n-1} \equiv 0 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ z-c) \]

would hold without all the $y_s$ being divisible by $z-c$.
