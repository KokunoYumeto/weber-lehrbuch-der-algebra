\gesperrt{assume, and $\varrho$ runs, according as $\alpha^{-1}\beta$ is a quadratic residue or a non-residue, in one system through the sequence of the residues or of the non-residues.} The change of the sign of $\xi$ gives no new substitution $W$. Accordingly we can represent $W$ in this way:

\weq{21}{W=\begin{pmatrix}\xi, & \xi\varrho\\ \xi^{-1}\varrho^{-1}(m-1), & \xi^{-1}m\end{pmatrix}.}

Now since the square of $W$

\[ W^2=\begin{pmatrix}\xi^2+m-1, & (\xi^2+m)\varrho\\ \xi^{-2}\varrho^{-1}(m-1)(\xi^2+m), & [(m-1)\xi^2+m^2]\xi^{-2}\end{pmatrix} \]

must belong to $\mathfrak{K}$, it is contained under one of the systems (13), (14), (16), (17), from which the following four possibilities result:

\weq{22}{\begin{aligned}
1.&\quad \xi^2+m\equiv 0,\\
2.&\quad \xi^2+m-1\equiv 0,\quad (m-1)\xi^2+m^2\equiv 0,\\
3.&\quad (\xi^2+m-1)[(m-1)\xi^2+m^2]\equiv m\xi^2,\\
4.&\quad (\xi^2+m-1)[(m-1)\xi^2+m^2]\equiv m'\xi^2,
\end{aligned}}

and each of the $p-1$ values (20), put for $\xi$, must satisfy one of these four cases. If one of the congruences (22), 2. is fulfilled, then the other must follow from it, which is possible only if

\weq{23}{2m\equiv 1,\qquad \xi^2\equiv m\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ p).}

Now a congruence with respect to a prime modulus has \gesperrt{at most} as many incongruent roots as the degree of the congruence amounts to; 1. and 2. can therefore be satisfied for at most two values each, 3. and 4. for at most four values each of $\xi$; there are therefore in all at most twelve values of $\xi$ which satisfy \gesperrt{one} of the four congruences (22).

From this it follows:

\[ p-1\leqq 12,\qquad p\leqq 13. \]

But if $p=13$, then each of the congruences (22) must have the maximum number of roots; 2. must therefore also be satisfied for two values of $\xi$; then by (23) we should have $m\equiv 7$, $\xi^2\equiv 7\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 13)$, which is impossible, since $7$ is a quadratic non-residue of $13$.

\gesperrt{A divisor of $\mathfrak{L}_0$ of index $p$ therefore does not exist} when $p>11$.
