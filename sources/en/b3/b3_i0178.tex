We now set, with \gesperrt{Jacobi},

\weq{3}{\Pi(u,v) = \int_0^u \dfrac{\varkappa^2\,\mathrm{sn}^2u\,\mathrm{sn}\,v\,\mathrm{cn}\,v\,\mathrm{dn}\,v}{1-\varkappa^2\,\mathrm{sn}^2u\,\mathrm{sn}^2v}\,du,}

and call $\Pi(u,v)$ \gesperrt{the transcendent of the third
kind} with the argument $u$ and the parameter $v$. If one
replaces $du$ by the expression:

\[
\dfrac{d\xi}{2\sqrt{\xi(1-\xi)(1-\varkappa^2\xi)}}
\]

and $\mathrm{sn}^2u$ by $\xi$, then both (1) and (2) yield an
elliptic integral of the third kind, such as we became acquainted
with in § 11.

From (1) there follows first of all

\weq{4}{\Pi(u,v)-u\,Z(v) = \Pi(v,u)-v\,Z(u),}

or \gesperrt{Jacobi's theorem} on the \gesperrt{interchange of argument
and parameter}.

The function $\Pi(u,v)$ vanishes when $v=K$ or
$v=K+iK'$, because for the former value $\mathrm{cn}\,v$ is equal to
zero, and for the second $\mathrm{dn}\,v$. If we therefore substitute
these values for $u$ in (4), there follows

\weq{5}{\begin{aligned}
\Pi(K,v) &= K\,Z(v)\\
\Pi(K+iK',v) &= (K+iK')\,Z(v)+\dfrac{i\pi v}{2K}\ [\text{§ 47, (13)}],
\end{aligned}}

by which the complete integrals of the third kind are reduced to the
second kind.
