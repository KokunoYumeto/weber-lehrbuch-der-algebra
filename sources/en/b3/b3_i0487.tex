\gesperrt{According to the results of \S\,122, perhaps with a finite number of exceptions,}

\begin{quote}
\gesperrt{a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of the field $\Omega$ is decomposable in the field $\mathfrak{K}(\Delta)$ into prime ideals of the first degree then and only then, when $\mathfrak{p}$ is of the first degree in $\Omega$ and belongs to the principal class, and is therefore equal to a complex prime}
\[
\pi = \frac{x+y\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}
\]
.
\end{quote}

If $\mathfrak{p}$ is to be decomposed in $\mathfrak{K}(D)$ into prime ideals $\mathfrak{P}$ of the first degree, there is the further requirement that $\pi$ belong to the order determined by $Q$, i.\,e. that it be congruent to a rational number with respect to the modulus $Q$ (\S\,96).
