For $p = 7$, since the interchange of $m$ with $m'$ yields nothing new, the following three possibilities remain: 1. $m \equiv 2$, $m' \equiv 6$. 2. $m \equiv 3$, $m' \equiv 5$. 3. $m \equiv 4$, $m' \equiv 4$.

In case 1., of the congruences § 81, (22), numbers 1. and 2. are impossible; hence for every $\xi$ one of the two congruences 3., 4. would have to be satisfied, which here read:
\[
(\xi^2 + 1)(\xi^2 + 4) \equiv 2\xi^2, 6\xi^2 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ 7),
\]
\gesperrt{none of which is satisfied for $\xi \equiv 1$.}

In case 3., (22), 1. cannot be satisfied, and (22), 2. is satisfied for $\xi^2 \equiv 4$; hence for $\xi^2 \equiv 1, 2$ the congruence (22) 3. or 4.:
\[
(\xi^2 + 3)(3\xi^2 + 2) \equiv 4\xi^2 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ 7)
\]
must be satisfied, which again is not the case. Thus for $p = 7$ there remains only:
\weq{5}{m \equiv 3, \quad m' \equiv 5 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ 7).}

For $p = 11$ the possibility that the congruences (22), 2. be satisfied is to be excluded from the outset, because in that case, in consequence of (4) and § 81, (23):
\[
m \equiv m'
\]
would have to hold; then (22), 3., 4. would not be distinct, and the congruences (22) could together have at most eight roots and not ten, as they nevertheless must.

Accordingly the congruences (22), 1., 3., 4 must each have the maximal number of roots, and in order that 1. be satisfied, $m$, and for the same reasons $m'$, must be a quadratic non-residue of 11. But this condition, and at the same time the condition (4), is satisfied only by the two numbers
\weq{6}{m, m' \equiv 2, -1 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ 11).}

The groups sought therefore consist, in these two cases, if they exist, of the following substitutions:

$p = 7$
\weq{7}{\begin{pmatrix} \xi, & 0 \\ 0, & \xi^{-1} \end{pmatrix}, \ \begin{pmatrix} 0, & \xi \\ \xi^{-1}, & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \ \begin{pmatrix} \xi & \xi\varrho \\ 2\xi^{-1}\varrho^{-1}, & 3\xi^{-1} \end{pmatrix}, \ \begin{pmatrix} -\xi\varrho, & \xi \\ -3\xi^{-1}, & 2\xi^{-1}\varrho^{-1} \end{pmatrix}}
\[
\xi \equiv 1, 2, 3 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ 7),
\]

$p = 11$
\weq{8}{\begin{pmatrix} \xi, & 0 \\ 0, & \xi^{-1} \end{pmatrix}, \ \begin{pmatrix} 0, & \xi \\ -\xi^{-1}, & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \ \begin{pmatrix} \xi, & \xi\varrho \\ \xi^{-1}\varrho^{-1}, & 2\xi^{-1} \end{pmatrix}, \ \begin{pmatrix} -\xi\varrho, & \xi \\ -2\xi^{-1}, & \xi^{-1}\varrho^{-1} \end{pmatrix}}
\[
\xi \equiv 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 \quad (\mathrm{mod}\ 11);
\]
