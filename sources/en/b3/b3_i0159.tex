Equation (5) can be written in the following three forms:
\weq{6}{\begin{aligned}
d\sqrt{\zeta} &= \pi\vartheta^2_{00}\sqrt{(1-\zeta)(1-\varkappa^2\zeta)}\,du,\\
d\sqrt{1-\zeta} &= -\pi\vartheta^2_{00}\sqrt{\zeta(1-\varkappa^2\zeta)}\,du,\\
d\sqrt{1-\varkappa^2\zeta} &= -\pi\vartheta^2_{00}\varkappa^2\sqrt{\zeta(1-\zeta)}\,du.
\end{aligned}}
If we introduce a new variable \(v\) by the equation
\weq{7}{\pi\vartheta^2_{00}u = v,}
then equations (5), (6) become:
\weq{8}{v = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\zeta} \frac{d\zeta}{\sqrt{\zeta(1-\zeta)(1-\varkappa^2\zeta)}},}
\weq{9}{\begin{aligned}
d\sqrt{\zeta} &= \sqrt{(1-\zeta)(1-\varkappa^2\zeta)}\,dv,\\
d\sqrt{1-\zeta} &= -\sqrt{\zeta(1-\varkappa^2\zeta)}\,dv,\\
d\sqrt{1-\varkappa^2\zeta} &= -\varkappa^2\sqrt{\zeta(1-\zeta)}\,dv,
\end{aligned}}
and now we regard the three quantities \(\sqrt{\zeta} = x\), \(\sqrt{1-\zeta} = y\),
\(\sqrt{1-\varkappa^2\zeta} = z\), defined by (4) and (7), as functions of the variable
\(v\). By (4) they are \gesperrt{one-valued, doubly periodic} and, apart from isolated
points at which they become infinite, \gesperrt{continuous} functions of \(v\). After
\gesperrt{Jacobi} they are called sinus amplitudinis, cosinus amplitudinis,
\(\varDelta\) amplitudinis of \(v\), and are denoted by \(\mathrm{sinam}\,v\),
\(\mathrm{cosam}\,v\), \(\varDelta\,\mathrm{am}\,v\). Here we wish to make use of the
shorter \gesperrt{Gudermann} notation \(\mathrm{sn}\,v\), \(\mathrm{cn}\,v\),
\(\mathrm{dn}\,v\), already mentioned in \S\,14, according to which
\weq{10}{\begin{aligned}
\frac{\vartheta_{11}(u)}{\vartheta_{01}(u)} &= \sqrt{\varkappa}\,\mathrm{sn}\,v,\\
\frac{\vartheta_{10}(u)}{\vartheta_{01}(u)} &= \sqrt{\frac{\varkappa}{\varkappa'}}\,\mathrm{cn}\,v,\\
\frac{\vartheta_{00}(u)}{\vartheta_{01}(u)} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{\varkappa'}}\,\mathrm{dn}\,v.
\end{aligned}}
