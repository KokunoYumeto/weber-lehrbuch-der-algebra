For a positive discriminant there are infinite families of associated
numbers, and the point is to single out a definite one from each of
these families by a suitable determination. For this the general
considerations of Bd.~II, \S\ 195 furnish us the means, which we now
apply to our special case.

Let, according to \S\ 98,
\[
\lambda = a\, t_1 + \frac{b+\sqrt{D}}{2}\, t_2
\]
be a basis form in the order $[Q]$ of an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, which we
shall assume to be \gesperrt{primary}, and let
\weq{4}{\begin{aligned}
y &= a\, x_1 + \frac{b+\sqrt{D}}{2}\, x_2 \\
y' &= a\, x_1 + \frac{b-\sqrt{D}}{2}\, x_2
\end{aligned}}
with integral rational $x_1$, $x_2$ be an integer $y$ in $[Q]$
divisible by $\mathfrak{a}$ and of positive norm, together with its
conjugate $y'$; and since we have assumed $\mathfrak{a}$ to be primary,
we have
\weq{5}{a = N(\mathfrak{a}).}
Let further
\weq{6}{\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon &= \frac{T+U\sqrt{D}}{2}, \\
\varepsilon' &= \frac{T-U\sqrt{D}}{2}
\end{aligned}}
be the fundamental unit in $O$ together with its conjugate, both of
which are \gesperrt{positive} and have positive norm.

We determine the numbers $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ from the linear
equations:
\weq{7}{\begin{aligned}
\xi_1 \log \varepsilon + \xi_2 &= \log |y|, \\
\xi_1 \log \varepsilon' + \xi_2 &= \log |y'|, \\
\xi_1 \log \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon'} &= \log \left| \frac{y}{y'} \right|, \\
\xi_2 &= \tfrac{1}{2} \log |y y'|,
\end{aligned}}
where $|y|$ denotes the absolute value of $y$.
