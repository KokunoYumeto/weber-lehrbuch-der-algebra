\begin{center}
\S\,195. \textbf{\gesperrt{Representation of the Differential Quotients by Polygon Quotients.}}
\end{center}

We take any function $z$ of the $n$th order in $\Omega$ and a minimal basis $\omega_1, \omega_2, \ldots, \omega_n$ with respect to $z$. All integral functions of $z$ of the field $\Omega$ are then representable in the form:
\weq{1}{\omega = x_1\omega_1 + x_2\omega_2 + \cdots + x_n\omega_n}
and arise from the basis functional
\weq{2}{\tau = t_1\omega_1 + t_2\omega_2 + \cdots + t_n\omega_n}
when integral rational functions $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ of $z$ are put for the functional variables $t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_n$.

If we put, as in \S\,184,
\[
N(t - \tau) = F(t, z),
\]
then $\tau$ satisfies the irreducible rational equation:
\[
F(\tau, z) = 0,
\]
and one obtains, by \S\,194, 3.:
\weq{3}{F'(\tau)\,d\tau + F'(z)\,dz = 0,}
and here $F'(\tau)$ is the \gesperrt{branching functional} (\S\,184).

By (1) and (2),
\weq{4}{d\omega = d\tau + \omega_1\,dx_1 + \omega_2\,dx_2 + \cdots + \omega_n\,dx_n,}
where in $d\tau$ the $t_i$ are to be replaced by the $x_i$, and from (3) there follows therefore the theorem:

4. \gesperrt{If $\omega$ is an integral function of $z$, then}
\weq{5}{F'(\tau)\frac{d\omega}{dz} = -F'(z) + \omega_1\frac{dx_1}{dz} + \cdots + \omega_n\frac{dx_n}{dz}}
