\begin{center}
\textbf{§ 201. The Residues.}
\end{center}

If $\mathfrak{P}$ is a point which divides $m$ times into the lower polygon $\mathfrak{B}$ of a differential $dJ$ ($m \mathrel{\underset{>}{=}} 0$),
then one takes a function $z$ in $\Omega$ which becomes infinitely large in the first order at $\mathfrak{P}$. Then, by § 197, (1) and § 185, (5), one may put:
\weq{1}{\frac{dJ}{dz} = a_{m-2}z^{m-2} + a_{m-3}z^{m-3} + \cdots + a_0 + a_{-1}z^{-1} + \eta z^{-2},}
in which the $a$ are constants, and $\eta$ is a function in $\Omega$ that is finite at $\mathfrak{P}$.

\gesperrt{The coefficient $-a_{-1}$ of $-z^{-1}$ is called the residue of the differential $dJ$ with respect to the point $\mathfrak{P}$.}

The residue of $dJ$ at the point $\mathfrak{P}$ can be different from zero only if $m>0$, that is, if the point $\mathfrak{P}$ occurs in the lower polygon of $dJ$. Such points shall be called poles of $dJ$.

1. \gesperrt{The residue of a proper differential is equal to zero. For by differentiation of a power of $z$, $z^{-1}$ can never arise.}

If for $z$ one takes a function $z_1$ which likewise becomes infinite of the first order at $\mathfrak{P}$, then one may put:
\[
z = cz_1 + \eta_1, \quad z^{-1} = c^{-1}z_1^{-1} + \eta_2,
\]
\weq{2}{\frac{dz}{dz_1} = c + z_1^{-2}\eta_3,}
in which the $c$ are constants, and $\eta_1, \eta_2, \eta_3$ are finite at $\mathfrak{P}$.

If one puts
\[
\frac{a_{m-2}z^{m-1}}{m-1} + \frac{a_{m-3}z^{m-2}}{m-2} + \cdots + a_0 = \omega,
% [sic] printed last term $a_0$ in the expression for $\omega$, should be $a_0 z$: only then is $d\omega/dz = a_{m-2}z^{m-2} + \cdots + a_0$, as the following display for $dJ/dz_1$ requires
\]
then from (1) there results:
\[
\frac{dJ}{dz_1} = \frac{d\omega}{dz_1} + a_{-1}z^{-1}\frac{dz}{dz_1} + z^{-2}\eta',
\]
where $\eta'$ remains finite at $\mathfrak{P}$, and in it, by 1., only the part
\[
a_{-1}z^{-1}\frac{dz}{dz_1}
\]
