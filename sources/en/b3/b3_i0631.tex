In this field let $A$ be a congruence group with the modulus
$M$ and the excludent $S$. The group $A$ contains, as we have
 seen, infinitely many \gesperrt{integers}. We ask for the number
$T(t)$ of the integers in $A$ that are divisible by some
given integral ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ coprime to $M$, and whose
norm is not greater than the positive number $t$.

Let $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ be a basis of $\mathfrak{m}$; then by \S\,91, (11):
\weq{1}{\alpha_1 = a_{22}\,a, \quad \alpha_2 = a_{22}\,\dfrac{b + \sqrt{\Delta}}{2}.}

Here let $a_{22}$ denote the greatest integral rational
number dividing $\mathfrak{m}$, and $a_{22}\,a$ the least integral rational number
divisible by $\mathfrak{m}$; $b$ is a root of the congruence:
\[
b^2 \equiv \Delta \pmod{4a},
\]
and we have:
\weq{2}{\Delta = b^2 - 4ac.}
\weq{3}{N(\mathfrak{m}) = a_{22}^2\,a.}
Since $\mathfrak{m}$ has been assumed relatively prime to $M$, we can, by
\S\,161, (6), determine in every number class with respect to $M$
occurring in $A$ a number $\alpha_0$ divisible by $\mathfrak{m}$. This number has the form
\[
\alpha_0 = x_0\alpha_1 + y_0\alpha_2,
\]
and if we set
\weq{4}{\begin{aligned} \alpha &= \alpha_0 + M(x\alpha_1 + y\alpha_2) \\ &= (x_0 + Mx)\alpha_1 + (y_0 + My)\alpha_2 \end{aligned}}
and let $x, y$ run through all positive and negative integral
rational numbers, then $\alpha$ runs through the totality of the
integers of the group $A$ that are divisible by $\mathfrak{m}$ and congruent
to $\alpha_0$ with respect to $M$.

In order to obtain all the integers in $A$ divisible by $\mathfrak{m}$,
we have to seek a complete system of residues with respect to $M$ in $\Omega$
and to select from among them the numbers $\alpha_0$ contained in $A$
that are divisible by $\mathfrak{m}$.

If the norm of this number $\alpha$ is to be not greater than $t$, then,
 if we denote the quadratic form
\[
a x^2 + b x y + c y^2
\]
by $\varphi(x, y)$, the condition for this is:
\[
\varphi(x_0 + Mx, \; y_0 + My) \leqq \dfrac{t}{a_{22}^2\,a}, \qquad [\S\,91, (2), (4)].
\]
