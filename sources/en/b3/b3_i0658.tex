The transition from one basis $\eta_1, \eta_2, \ldots, \eta_n$ to another $\eta_1', \eta_2', \ldots, \eta_n'$ takes place through a linear substitution:
\weq{9}{(\eta_1', \eta_2', \ldots, \eta_n') = L(\eta_1, \eta_2, \ldots, \eta_n)}
or, written more briefly,
\weq{10}{(\eta_i') = L(\eta_i),}
in which $L$ has the meaning
\weq{11}{
L = \begin{pmatrix}
l_{1,1}, & l_{1,2}, & \ldots, & l_{1,n} \\
l_{2,1}, & l_{2,2}, & \ldots, & l_{2,n} \\
\cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\
l_{n,1}, & l_{n,2}, & \ldots, & l_{n,n}
\end{pmatrix} \quad \text{(Bd.\,II, \S\,41).}
}

The elements $l_{i,k}$ of this substitution are rational functions of $z$.

\gesperrt{The system $(\eta')$ is again a basis then and only then, when the determinant $|L|$ of this substitution does not vanish.}

\zeta\eta_2 &= y_{2,1}\eta_1 + y_{2,2}\eta_2 + \cdots + y_{2,n}\eta_n, \\
