Every substitution $S$ can be derived by composition of

\[
\begin{pmatrix} m, & 0 \\ 0, & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\]

with a substitution $T$; in order that

\[
S = \begin{pmatrix} m, & 0 \\ 0, & 1 \end{pmatrix} T
\]

hold, one has simply to determine $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ from the congruences

\[
a \equiv \alpha m, \quad b \equiv \beta m, \quad c \equiv \gamma, \quad d \equiv \delta \pmod{n}
\]

We now consider

\weq{10}{x_{\mu,\mu'} = \mathrm{sn}\left(\frac{4\mu K + 4\mu' i K'}{n}\right) = \frac{\vartheta_{00}\,\vartheta_{11}\left(\dfrac{2\mu + 2\mu'\omega}{n}\right)}{\vartheta_{10}\,\vartheta_{01}\left(\dfrac{2\mu + 2\mu'\omega}{n}\right)},}

\[
\varkappa^2 = \frac{\vartheta_{10}^4}{\vartheta_{00}^4}
\]

as functions of $\omega$, and apply to them a linear transformation

\[
\begin{pmatrix} \alpha, & \beta \\ \gamma, & \delta \end{pmatrix} \equiv \begin{pmatrix} 1, & 0 \\ 0, & 1 \end{pmatrix} \pmod{8}
\]

in which we replace $\omega$ by

\weq{11}{\omega' = \frac{\gamma + \delta\omega}{\alpha + \beta\omega}}

Then, by the formulae (3), (5) and (6), \S\, 39, it follows that

\[
x_{\mu,\mu'} \quad \text{into} \quad x_{\alpha\mu + \gamma\mu',\,\beta\mu + \delta\mu'}
\]

passes over; that is, the $x_{\mu,\mu'}$ undergo a substitution which, by (3), (6), is to be denoted by

\[
T = \begin{pmatrix} \delta, & -\gamma \\ -\beta, & \alpha \end{pmatrix}
\]

while $\varkappa^2$ remains unchanged.

Conversely, every one of the substitutions $T$ can arise in this way as well; for if any substitution $T$ whatever is given, then by adding suitable multiples of the (odd) number $n$ to the numbers $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ the conditions

\[
\alpha \equiv 1, \quad \delta \equiv 1, \quad \beta \equiv 0, \quad \gamma \equiv 0 \pmod{8}
\]

can always be satisfied.
