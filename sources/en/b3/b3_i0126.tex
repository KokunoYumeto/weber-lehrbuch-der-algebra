\begin{center}
\textbf{§ 32. \gesperrt{The principal transformations of the second order of the $\vartheta$-functions.}}
\end{center}

The two principal transformations of order $n$
\[
\begin{pmatrix} 1, & 0 \\ 0, & n \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} n, & 0 \\ 0, & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\]
transform, by § 27, (11), the characteristic $(g_1, g_2)$ into
\[
(ng_1, g_2), \quad (g_1, ng_2),
\]
that is, for odd $n$ the characteristic remains unchanged, while for even $n$ it passes over into
\weq{1}{(0, g_2) \text{ or } (g_1, 0).}
Since the transformation of even degree accordingly behaves essentially otherwise than that of odd degree, we consider first the case $n = 2$. The first and second principal transformations of the second degree are called the \gesperrt{Landen} and the \gesperrt{Gauss} transformations.

By § 27, (12),
\weq{2}{\begin{aligned}
\vartheta_{g_1, g_2}\left(u, \frac{\omega}{2}\right) &= \Theta_{g_1, 0}(u, \omega) \\
\vartheta_{g_1, g_2}(2u, 2\omega) &= \Theta_{0, g_2}(u, \omega)
\end{aligned}}
are $\Theta$-functions of the second order in $u, \omega$, which admit of representation according to § 21.

We obtain first the two pairs of formulae in which $A, B$ are independent of $u$:
\weq{3}{\begin{aligned}
A\vartheta_{11}\left(u, \frac{\omega}{2}\right) &= \vartheta_{01}(u, \omega)\vartheta_{11}(u, \omega), \\
A\vartheta_{10}\left(u, \frac{\omega}{2}\right) &= \vartheta_{00}(u, \omega)\vartheta_{10}(u, \omega),
\end{aligned}}
\weq{4}{\begin{aligned}
B\vartheta_{11}(2u, 2\omega) &= \vartheta_{10}(u, \omega)\vartheta_{11}(u, \omega), \\
B\vartheta_{01}(2u, 2\omega) &= \vartheta_{00}(u, \omega)\vartheta_{01}(u, \omega),
\end{aligned}}
