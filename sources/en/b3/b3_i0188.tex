We saw in the previous section that, if

\weq{3}{\varkappa^2 = \dfrac{\vartheta_{10}^4}{\vartheta_{00}^4}}

holds, this problem is solved by the elliptic functions, and indeed in such a way that $x$, $y$, $z$ become single-valued functions of $v$ in the whole $v$-plane, continuous except where they are infinite. But if $\varkappa^2$ is now given, the question arises whether for every value of $\varkappa^2$ there is a value of $\omega$ satisfying the condition (3), and whether there are several such values.

We prove first that the functions $x$, $y$, $z$ are \gesperrt{uniquely} determined by the differential equations (1) together with the subsidiary conditions.

It follows first from (1) that $x^2+y^2$, $\varkappa^2x^2+z^2$ are constant, and from the subsidiary conditions there follows:

\weq{4}{y^2 = 1-x^2, \qquad z^2 = 1-\varkappa^2x^2.}

Suppose that a second system of functions $x_1$, $y_1$, $z_1$ satisfying the same conditions exists; then first

\[
y_1^2 = 1-x_1^2, \qquad z_1^2 = 1-\varkappa^2x_1^2,
\]

and a simple differentiation shows that

\[
\frac{x\,y_1z_1 - x_1\,yz}{1-\varkappa^2x^2x_1^2}
\]

is constant. (The addition theorem of the elliptic integrals, from which one obtains the form of this expression, is confirmed directly by differentiation.) But from the subsidiary conditions it follows that this constant vanishes, and hence:

\[
x\,y_1z_1 = x_1\,yz,
\]

from which one easily concludes, by squaring both sides:

\[
x = x_1, \qquad y=y_1, \qquad z=z_1.
\]

\begin{center}
\textbf{\S\,52. \gesperrt{The independent variable $\varkappa^2$. Linear differential equation for $K$.}}
\end{center}

We now show first that, and how, for an arbitrarily given value of $\varkappa^2$ one can find at least \gesperrt{one} value of $\omega$ satisfying the condition

\weq{1}{\varkappa^2 = \dfrac{\vartheta_{10}^4}{\vartheta_{00}^4}}

This question is answered most completely by reduction to a linear differential equation of the second order.
